# Clean Task Skill Tree

## Goal Identification
- Parse the task: "clean some [object] and put it in/on [destination]" or "put a clean [object] in/on [destination]".
- The [object] must be cleaned before placing at [destination].
- Identify [cleaning_source] as a sinkbasin or bathtubbasin (typically sinkbasin 1).

## Phase 1: Locate and Take the Target Object
### Systematic Search Order – Follow Exactly
- Read the initial room description. Identify every receptacle type present: cabinets, drawers, fridge, countertops, diningtables, sidetables, shelves, sinkbasins, etc.
- **Search in this exact order and do NOT skip any step:**
  1. **All cabinets** in increasing number order (cabinet 1, cabinet 2, ...). For each, first go to it, then `open cabinet X` to inspect contents. Do not use 'look' or 'examine' – only `open` reveals contents of closed containers.
  2. **All drawers** in increasing number order (drawer 1, drawer 2, ...). For each, go, `open drawer X`, inspect.
  3. **The fridge** (if present). Go, `open fridge 1`, inspect.
  4. **All open surfaces** (countertops, diningtables, sidetables, shelves, sinkbasins, etc.) in any order. You can see objects on open surfaces without opening – just go to them and read the observation. **After checking cabinets and drawers (and fridge if present), you MUST check every open surface. Do not stop after step 3.**
- **Important**: Always open closed containers (cabinets, drawers, fridge) after arriving. Do not use `look` or `examine` – they do not reveal contents of closed containers. Use `open` instead.
- If you have searched all of the above and still not found the target object, re-read the initial description to ensure you did not miss a receptacle type (e.g., a shelf or bathtub).

### Navigation First: Move Before Interacting
- **Before attempting to open any container or take any object, you must be at that exact location.**
- The sequence is always: `go to <receptacle>` then `open <receptacle>` or `take <object>`. If you try to open or take from a distance, the action will only move you there and not actually perform the operation.
- Pay attention to observations: if you see "You arrive at ..." instead of "You open ...", you were not at the right place. Correct by issuing the `open` again after moving.

### Always Open Containers Upon Arrival
- When you arrive at a closed container (cabinet, drawer, fridge), immediately open it to see its contents. Do not assume it is open or that you can see inside without opening.
- This applies even to the first cabinet (cabinet 1) – after going to it, always open it.

### Persistence – Do Not Stop Early
- **The search is not complete until you have visited every single instance of every receptacle type present.** Stopping before finishing all types is the most common cause of failure.
- After completing the systematic order above, if you still have not found the object, re-read the initial description – you may have missed a receptacle type.
- Avoid redundant 'look' or 'examine' actions that waste steps and do not help find objects. Only 'open' works for closed containers.

### Object Name Verification
- Before taking an object, verify that its name matches the task target **exactly** (e.g., "cup" ≠ "mug"; "pan" ≠ "pot"). If it matches, take it and proceed to Phase 2. If not, continue searching.

## Phase 2: Clean the Object
- While holding [object], go to [cleaning_source] (typically sinkbasin 1).
- Immediately use "clean [object] with [cleaning_source]".
- Confirm the observation says you cleaned it; if not retry or check you are at the correct source.
- Do not use "look" or "examine" at the sink – only the clean action works.

## Phase 3: Place the Clean Object at Destination
- After cleaning, go to the [destination] specified in the task (e.g., cabinet, diningtable, microwave, stoveburner).
- Use "move [object] to [destination]" to place it.
- Confirm the observation shows the object is now at the destination.

## Completion
- The task ends when you have successfully placed the clean object. Do not continue searching or moving other objects.
