# pick_and_place skill tree

## Goal
Pick up a specified object and place it into or onto a specified receptacle.

## Sub-Goal Flow
1. **Extract the full list of every receptacle** (noun + number) from the initial observation. If the observation is truncated (ends abruptly), use `look` once *before* any movement to get the full list. Mentally note the list.
2. **Check the target receptacle first** – go to it, open if closed, inspect contents. If the target object is there, take it and skip to Placement.
3. **If not found, systematically visit every other receptacle on your list, one by one, without revisiting any. Do not stop or move to another room until every receptacle on that list has been visited.**
4. **For each receptacle:**
   - If closed, `open` it immediately (next action must be `open`). Do **not** use `examine`, `look`, `inventory`, or any other action first.
   - Inspect contents. If the target is present, take it and go to Placement.
   - Leave it open; do not close.
5. **After visiting all receptacles** and still not finding the object, the object may be in another room – go to a doorway and repeat the search.

## Object Search (Mandatory)

### Step 1: Extract all receptacles (BEFORE any movement)
- Every noun phrase followed by a number (e.g., bed 1, drawer 2, shelf 3, cabinet 4, bathtubbasin 1, toilet 1, sink 1, table 1, countertop 1, armchair 1, sofa 1, dresser 1, desk 1, sidetable 1, etc.) is a potential location. Include **all** types: beds, shelves, drawers, cabinets, tables, counters, sofas, armchairs, dressers, garbage cans, holders, basins, toilets – any furniture item with a number.
- **Critical:** If initial observation is cut off (e.g., ends with "a sideta..."), you **must** use `look` once before any `go to` action. Then avoid further `look` or `inventory` during search.

### Step 2: Create a mental checklist and follow it strictly
- Write down (mentally) every receptacle exactly as seen, e.g., "armchair 1, bed 1, bed 2, cabinet 1, desk 1, desk 2, drawer 1, drawer 2, ... dresser 1, garbagecan 1".
- Starting with the first receptacle on the list (after checking the target receptacle), go to it. Do **not** skip any. If you are unsure of the next, re-read the initial observation from the prompt to refresh the list.
- **Every closed container must be opened immediately upon arrival** – your next action after `go to` must be `open`. Do not `examine` or `look` first.
- After opening, if the target object is not present, immediately move to the next receptacle on your list. Do not revisit any receptacle until you have been through the entire list.
- **If you catch yourself moving to an already-visited place**, stop and consult your list to pick the next unvisited one.
- **Never conclude the object is missing** until you have visited every single receptacle on your list (including any you might have initially overlooked like desks, countertops, armchairs, sofas).

### Common pitfalls leading to failed search
- **Stopping early** – even if you have searched many locations, you must finish the entire list. The object could be on a shelf, desk, or in a drawer you haven't checked.
- **Assuming certain types are irrelevant** – small objects can be on any surface; do not exclude desks, countertops, armchairs, sofas, or any furniture.
- **Revisiting instead of advancing** – once visited, a receptacle is done; there is no need to check it again.
- **Not opening closed containers** – you must open them to see inside; `examine` does not reveal contents.

## Object Acquisition
- **Name match:** Only take an object if its exact base noun (without number) matches the target. "Knife" is not "butterknife".
- **Action:** `take <object> from <receptacle>`.

## Placement
- Go to the target receptacle. If closed, open it. Then `move <object> to <receptacle>`.
- **Stop immediately after the move action** – do not perform any further actions.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid (repeated for emphasis)
- Not extracting all receptacles before moving.
- Not opening closed containers on arrival.
- Revisiting already-checked receptacles.
- Stopping search early.
- Taking without exact name match.
- Unnecessary `look` or `inventory`.
